# Sights of Space — A SpaceEngine Visual Portfolio

A curated collection of space visualizations made while exploring astronomical objects and planetary systems in **SpaceEngine**. This small gallery reflects my curiosity about the scale, variety, and physics of the universe.

> **Credit:** SpaceEngine © Cosmographic Software LLC.

## Gallery

### 1. Earth and Moon
![Earth and Moon, as rendered in SpaceEngine](images/earth-and-moon.jpg)

A nearby reminder of our place in space: a view of the Earth–Moon system. The Moon is Earth's natural satellite and is tidally locked, so it keeps approximately the same face turned toward Earth.

### 2. The Pillars of Creation
![Pillars of Creation, as rendered in SpaceEngine](images/pillars-of-creation.jpg)

A visualization inspired by the Pillars of Creation, structures of gas and dust in the Eagle Nebula. Dense material in such regions can be associated with the formation and early evolution of stars.

### 3. The Carina Nebula
![Carina Nebula, as rendered in SpaceEngine](images/carina-nebula.jpg)

The Carina Nebula is a large, active star-forming region in the Milky Way. Radiation and stellar winds from massive young stars shape the surrounding gas and dust.

### 4. Saturn's Rings
![Saturn and its rings, as rendered in SpaceEngine](images/saturn-rings.jpg)

Saturn's rings are made mostly of countless particles of water ice, with rocky material and dust mixed in. They form a broad, thin system rather than a solid disc.

### 5. Among Saturn's Ring Particles
![A close perspective on Saturn's ring particles, as rendered in SpaceEngine](images/saturn-ring-particles.jpg)

A closer, perspective-driven visualization of ring material. Saturn's rings are dynamically complex: particles orbit Saturn, collide, and interact gravitationally with moons and one another.

### 6. Kepler-16b
![Kepler-16b, as rendered in SpaceEngine](images/kepler-16b.jpg)

Kepler-16b is a circumbinary planet: it orbits two stars rather than one. Its discovery helped show that planets can exist in systems with two suns.

### 7. TRAPPIST-1e
![TRAPPIST-1e, as rendered in SpaceEngine](images/trappist-1e.jpg)

TRAPPIST-1e is one of seven known Earth-sized planets orbiting the ultracool dwarf star TRAPPIST-1. Its actual surface conditions and appearance are not known; this image is a simulation, not a photograph of the planet.

### 8. Proxima Centauri b
![Proxima Centauri b, as rendered in SpaceEngine](images/proxima-centauri-b.jpg)

Proxima Centauri b is a planet orbiting Proxima Centauri, the closest star to the Sun. It is interesting for studies of nearby exoplanets, but its atmosphere, surface, and habitability remain uncertain. The rendered appearance shown here is illustrative.

### 9. Haumea, viewed from the direction of Namaka
![Haumea and its surroundings, as rendered in SpaceEngine](images/haumea-from-namaka.jpg)

A simulated viewpoint associated with Haumea, a rapidly rotating dwarf planet in the Kuiper Belt, and Namaka, one of its moons. This is a visualization of a distant system, not a spacecraft photograph.

### 10. The Crab Nebula
![Crab Nebula, as rendered in SpaceEngine](images/crab-nebula.jpg)

The Crab Nebula is the remnant of a supernova observed on Earth in 1054 CE. At its centre is a rapidly rotating neutron star known as the Crab Pulsar, which powers emission from the surrounding nebula.

## What I am exploring

- How stars form, evolve, and leave behind remnants
- Planetary systems, exoplanets, and the search for potentially habitable environments
- The structure of nebulae and supernova remnants
- How visualization can support curiosity and communicate astronomy

## Important note on scientific accuracy

These are **SpaceEngine-generated visualizations**, not original telescope photographs, spacecraft observations, or scientific measurements made by me. For exoplanets and distant objects, details such as surface colours, terrain, oceans, and atmospheric appearance may be simulated or artistically rendered and should not be taken as confirmed facts. The captions provide brief educational context; they are not a substitute for primary scientific sources.
